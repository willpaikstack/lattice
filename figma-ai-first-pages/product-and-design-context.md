# Product And Design Context

## Product

Lattice OS is a private manufacturing RFQ, quote, procurement, and supplier-network workflow platform.

The platform is for domestic companies and machine shops that need to outsource precision manufacturing work to a vetted overseas supplier network. The core use case is helping teams accept work they might otherwise no-quote because they lack capacity, machines, material availability, labor, or specific fabrication capabilities.

Lattice is not a generic SaaS project management tool. It is an operational manufacturing procurement system, closer in domain to Xometry, Fictiv, Hubs, or Protolabs, but with a private, relationship-driven, invite-only feel.

## Audience

Primary public-entry audience:

- Buyers at domestic companies or machine shops.
- Operations/procurement users who submit RFQs with CAD files and drawings.
- Existing invited users who need to log in quickly.
- Prospective teams who may join a waiting list.

These users should feel that Lattice is serious, controlled, manufacturing-literate, and trustworthy. The first screens should not feel like a generic startup landing page or a broad marketing website.

## Product Direction

The public entry point is intentionally invite-only. The visible choices should stay constrained:

- Log in
- Join waiting list / request access

Do not add open signup, pricing plans, chat widgets, public marketplace browsing, or broad marketing navigation.

The product UI is moving toward a light B2B operations console once logged in:

- neutral light backgrounds
- restrained accents
- dense but readable operational information
- manufacturing-specific language
- supplier-network trust signals
- clear RFQ/order/dashboard navigation

The public entry pages can be more visually immersive than the app shell, but they should still feel precise, restrained, and premium.

## Visual Taste

Aim for a result that feels designed by a high-end web product team at the level of Apple, Airbnb, Stripe, Linear, or Ramp, but adapted to manufacturing operations rather than consumer lifestyle or generic fintech.

Good directions:

- precise typography
- strong visual hierarchy
- quiet confidence
- excellent spacing
- real manufacturing imagery or richly designed manufacturing/product-system visuals
- no clutter
- premium but not flashy
- private access / controlled workspace feel
- subtle motion states and clear focus states if interaction states are shown

Avoid:

- generic SaaS gradients
- purple-blue startup palette
- abstract blobs/orbs
- crypto/AI dashboard aesthetics
- oversized marketing claims
- long navigation menus
- fake customer logos unless clearly placeholder
- generic factory stock-photo feel
- dark, illegible overlays that hide the actual subject

## Brand Notes

Current brand name: Lattice

Current logo mark: simple cube / lattice mark in a rounded square. You may redesign the surrounding page and supporting brand system, but keep the brand identity understated and precise.

The current public pages use a dark manufacturing photo background. Figma AI may replace this with a better first-viewport composition if it better supports the product story.
