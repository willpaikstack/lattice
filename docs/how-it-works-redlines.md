# How It Works — proposed redlines

Status: **items 4, 5, 6, 7, and 8 implemented on 2026-08-14; items 1, 2, 3, and 9 remain pending.**

Use the item numbers to approve or decline individual changes. Strikethrough is proposed removal; underlined text is proposed replacement.

## 1. Lead with the economic outcome in the hero

**Current**

> Lattice gives domestic manufacturers access to qualified global production capacity, helping shops fulfill overflow demand while keeping their customer relationships and existing production schedules intact.

**Proposed**

> ~~Lattice gives domestic manufacturers access to qualified global production capacity, helping shops fulfill overflow demand while keeping their customer relationships and existing production schedules intact.~~
>
> <ins>When your floor is full, Lattice gives you a managed path to qualified global production capacity—helping you accept more work without expanding headcount, equipment, or floor space in lockstep with demand.</ins>

Why: puts revenue and capital avoidance ahead of the mechanism.

## 2. Make the CTA destination match a backlog conversation

Keep the How It Works button as **“Talk to us about your backlog.”** Update the existing `/waiting-list` form:

| Current | Proposed |
| --- | --- |
| Waiting list request | <ins>Backlog evaluation</ins> |
| Request access to Lattice | <ins>Tell us what work you can’t fit on your floor.</ins> |
| Tell us who you are and what kind of manufacturing procurement work you want to bring into the workspace. | <ins>Share the type of work, approximate timing, and capacity constraint. We’ll follow up to discuss whether Lattice is a fit.</ins> |
| Procurement needs | <ins>What work needs coverage?</ins> |
| RFQs, production orders, supplier follow-up, materials, or parts you source. | <ins>Process or capability, approximate timing, material or quality requirements, and the capacity constraint.</ins> |
| Submit request | <ins>Start the conversation</ins> |

Why: the current form reads like software access, after the page has invited a manufacturing-capacity discussion.

## 3. Resolve the invite-only and “Send us the job” tension

**Current step title**

> Send us the job

**Proposed step title and opening copy**

> <ins>After approval, send us the job</ins>
>
> <ins>Approved accounts share the drawing, quantity, requirements, and delivery date.</ins>

Why: preserves the current invite-only policy without implying that a public visitor can upload a job immediately.

## 4. Make the global-capacity opening more measured — implemented

**Current**

> Finding an overseas machine shop is easy. Knowing whether it can consistently meet your drawings, material requirements, quality standards, and delivery commitments is not.

**Proposed**

> ~~Finding an overseas machine shop is easy.~~ <ins>Finding a supplier is only the first step.</ins> Knowing whether it can consistently meet your drawings, material requirements, quality standards, and delivery commitments <ins>requires qualification.</ins>

Why: keeps the credibility point while avoiding a line that can feel dismissive of the work involved.

## 5. Put the first-use guidance before the workflow — implemented

**Current order**

> How the workflow works → Start with predictable, lower-risk overflow work.

**Proposed order**

> <ins>Start with predictable, lower-risk overflow work.</ins> → <ins>How the workflow works</ins>

Why: gives a skeptical operator a practical first use case before asking them to absorb process details.

## 7. Merge the repeated closing CTA and invite-only note — implemented

**Current**

> A large CTA card is followed by a separate invite-only note that repeats the same next step.

**Proposed single CTA**

> Have work you can’t fit on the floor?
>
> <ins>Talk with Lattice about the backlog or overflow work you need to evaluate. Lattice is invite-only; we’ll help determine whether the network fits your requirements.</ins>
>
> Talk to us about your backlog

Why: retains the qualification signal while ending the page with one clear action.

## 8. Shorten the mobile header CTA and retain login access — implemented

**Desktop**: keep **“Talk to us about your backlog.”**

**Mobile proposed treatment**

> <ins>Talk to us</ins> (CTA) · <ins>Log in remains visible</ins>

Why: the full CTA crowds the narrow header and currently pushes the login link away on mobile.

## 9. Add a concise proof point only when the public policy is ready

**Proposed optional trust link, after the quality-review workflow step**

> <ins>See what documentation is available before shipment</ins>

This would link to a brief, public explanation of inspection reports, material certifications, and review expectations. **Deferred unless the underlying documentation policy is ready to publish.**

Why: the page correctly makes quality review a key trust moment; a concrete follow-on resource would help buyers validate the claim.
